# 0029: The kernel's remaining work, in the order the Vibrant Mindz site needs it

- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Status:** Accepted. The owner asked on 2026-11-16 for the Vibrant Mindz modules to come before anything else; this record is the order that takes. `KICKOFF.md` and `docs/HANDOFF.md` follow it.
- **Context:** The new Vibrant Mindz site (`docs/plans/vibrant-mindz-module-plan.md`) is waiting on DevKit. Its own sessions have written a backend plan, seven module scaffolds and a build order in eight steps (its step 0 brings them into this repo as `docs/plans/vibrant-mindz-backend/`), and they can't go further until the modules exist. `KICKOFF.md` finishes K2 (OAuth, API keys and invites, lifecycle) before K3. The site's modules can't run on K2 alone, though: they call `email.send`, `storage`, `events`, `jobs`, `audit`, `notify` and `moderation` (K3, SPEC 6), they mount through the web kernel (K4), and the site's kickoff checks every step with `devkit new` and `devkit check` (K5). Decision 0018 puts its ten contract hooks after the kernel phases and before blog is extracted, and blog is in the site's step 1.
- **Decision:**
  1. The rest of the kernel and the site's modules come in this order: the loose ends of K2 step 8 (step-up by TOTP and password re-checking the session, #52; the passkey reset gap once the owner answers); the site's step 0 (its scaffold, and items 1 and 2 of its `kernel-additions.md`); K3; K4, with the site's theme; K5; the ten hooks of decision 0018; then the site's steps 1 to 7.
  2. K2's last steps wait for the site's need of them. Step 10's invites come before the site's step 4 (the community ports VybeMindz's invites); API keys come after. Steps 9 (OAuth) and 11 (deletion with its grace period, data export, abuse protection, the remaining security mails) come before the site launches at its step 5. K2's gate (every SPEC 14.13 contract test passes) is checked then, and the kernel's "done when" with it.
  3. K3 builds first what the site's modules use: storage, email, jobs, events, audit, notifications, moderation and public rendering. Retention and the AI client follow when a module declares a need for them.
- **Alternatives:** Finish K2 first, as `KICKOFF.md` had it: three more sessions before anything the site needs. Build each kernel service only when the first module asks for it: the site's step 1 (media, pages, blog, embeds, contact, campaigns) uses nearly all of K3 at once, so it saves little, and decision 0018 already turned down adding hooks module by module.
- **Consequences:** Nothing in SPEC.md changes. K2's gate is checked later than `KICKOFF.md` first said, and the contract suite keeps its expected failures for OAuth, API keys and lifecycle until then. No site goes live on DevKit before that gate passes. The site's own kickoff assumes `devkit check` exists from its step 1, which this order makes true.
- **See:** `KICKOFF.md`; `docs/plans/vibrant-mindz-module-plan.md` section 11; decision 0018; `docs/HANDOFF.md`
