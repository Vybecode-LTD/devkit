# 0045: People and roles in the admin: who may grant and edit what, the locks that keep the last owner, and `auth.role.manage`

- **Date:** 2026-11-19
- **Status:** Decided; applied in K4.7 (#85). Amended 2026-11-19 (#88): a grant also emits `auth.user.role_changed` `{user_id, added, removed}` in its transaction, as SPEC 11 promised; K4.7 had only audited it.
- **Context:** K4.7 builds the admin's People and Roles to VM Website's `design/site/AdminPeople.dc.html` and `AdminRoles.dc.html` (K4 contract §2 additions 4 and 5, §4 item 7; decisions 0013, 0042 and 0044). SPEC 14.1 set who may grant what (decision 0013) and 14.8 that staff edit roles in the admin, but nothing had built the routes: `GET /api/auth/admin/users` and `PUT .../roles` were written down with contract tests marked not built since K2, and role editing was new in the K4 contract. Building them found:
  - Decision 0013's rule ("a role can be granted or removed only by someone who holds every permission it carries") would stop an admin granting `member` once any module gives members a permission admins don't hold. Suspensions already met this and treat `member` as no rank (decision 0039).
  - A role's permissions can change after people hold it, and a role can be deleted while someone is being given it. The last owner's guard counts owners, and two owners each stepping down at once take different account locks, so they could both count two.
  - The contract named no answer for an unknown role, and `kernel.not_found` is what the router answers for a route that doesn't exist, which the contract suite reads as not built.
  - The Roles page needs each permission's words. The backend can't read `kernel/kernel.json` when running (contract change 8), and the modules' manifests are read at start.
- **Decision:**
  1. **`auth.role.manage`** ("Create, edit and delete roles, within the permissions you hold"), admin by default, migration `kernel_0012`. Editing a role also needs step-up, as granting does.
  2. **Who may grant, remove or edit what.** Only the roles a change adds or removes are judged, so a role the caller couldn't grant may stay where it is. A role is granted or removed by someone holding every permission it carries; only an owner grants or removes `owner`; the last active owner keeps it. `member` is no rank: anyone with `auth.role.assign` grants or removes it, as with suspensions. A site's own role is made, changed or deleted only by someone holding every permission it has or would have. The built-in roles stay as the manifests declare them (409 `auth.role_builtin`); a role someone holds isn't deleted (409 `auth.role_in_use`).
  3. **The locks.** A grant locks the caller's and the person's rows in id order, as a suspension does, then `owner`'s role row for update when it removes `owner`, then takes a key-share lock on every other role it adds or removes. Two owners each stepping down at once meet on `owner`'s row, and the second counts after the first. Deleting a role locks its row for update, which a grant being written holds a key-share lock against, so the delete counts that holder after it commits, and a grant arriving after a delete finds the role gone (422 `auth.role_unknown`). Changing a role locks its row against other changes only, then raises its holders' role versions, so their next request carries a new token (14.5). Each race has a test that holds the moment open, and each failed with its lock taken out.
  4. **The answers** (docs/contracts/auth-api.md): the People list gains `name` (null until something sets one) and a `q` matched as typed, never as a pattern; a grant answers the person as the list does and is audited as `auth.user.roles_changed` with `{added, removed}`; an unknown role id is 422 `auth.role_unknown` in a grant and 404 `auth.role_not_found` when editing; role edits are audited as `auth.role.created`, `auth.role.changed` (`{added, removed}`) and `auth.role.deleted`, targeting `auth.role`.
  5. **The permissions list.** The kernel keeps its permissions' words in code (`KERNEL_PERMISSION_WORDS`, held to `kernel.json`'s keys, words and order by a test); the app lists them, then each installed module's in its manifest's order, as `{key, description, owner}`.
  6. **The screens.** People: search by address, the person's roles as pills by name, second factor (a warning for staff without one), last seen, standing, and Open for someone holding `auth.role.assign`, which switches roles on and off, a role the server would refuse drawn off with its reason. Roles: a card per role with how many hold it, the permissions in groups against the roles (the kernel's as People and access, Site and system, Moderation; a module's under its id), New role and Edit on the site's own roles for someone holding `auth.role.manage`. The sidebar's person card names roles from the roles read when the person may make it.
  7. **Left off, not faked:** Invite someone and the Invites tab (K2 step 10); Export; the Staff, Artists and Members chips (the list has no filter by role); the reminders (no route sends one); suspending and restoring from People; names; per-resource grants such as the board's "own".
- **Consequences:** a site's custom roles are real rows any module's permissions can be granted to; a module that gives members a permission doesn't block staff. The contract suite's admin tests, written in K2, run for real. Role editing raises role versions, so a busy role's holders all get new tokens on their next request.
