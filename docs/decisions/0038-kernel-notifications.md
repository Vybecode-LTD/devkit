# 0038: Kernel notifications: one template per key, each person's choice per key, and one-click unsubscribe

- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Status:** Applied
- **Context:** K3's sixth service (decision 0029). SPEC 6 promised `notify(user, key, context)`, "an in-site and email notice, sent by the person's preferences for that key, with one-click unsubscribe", and module.json already declared each key with a description and a default (`in_site`, `email`, `both`). The site's comments, community, orders and licenses all need it (the Vibrant Mindz module plan, section 10). KICKOFF pointed at Basefra.me's notification center for ideas; it turned out to be an owner-alert plugin, worth two ideas only: a notice never fails its caller's change for a delivery problem, and floods are the sender's to prevent. The owner approved the contract (`dev plans/contracts/k3-notifications.md`) on 2026-11-17 with the four answers recommended.
- **Decision:**
  1. **`notifications.notify(db, user_id, key, context, *, link=None) -> Sent`** renders the key's template now, then, in the caller's transaction, adds a `kernel_notification` row and queues the email (sealed, through `email.queue_message`), each as the person chose for that key, else as the manifest's default. It returns where the notice went. A caller's bug (an undeclared key, a link off the site, a missing value) is ValueError before anything is written.
  2. **One template per key.** Each declared key has an email template of the same name; its subject, rendered, is the notice's title in the site. `assemble` refuses a key without one, another module's key, and a key declared twice.
  3. **Who gets what.** Email only to an `active` account with a verified address; nothing to an account that isn't `active`. `link` is a path on the site (one slash, printable ASCII, no backslash or space, at most 500), given to the template as an absolute URL.
  4. **Preferences per key** (the owner's answer): a `kernel_notification_preference` row exists only once a person changes a key from its default. No global switch.
  5. **One-click unsubscribe** (the owner's answer: that key alone). Every notice email gets a footer, `kernel.notification_footer`, a template a site can reword, and RFC 8058's `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers; `Mailer.send` and `email.queue_message` gain `headers`, limited to those two, and `Checked` passes them on only when there are some. The token is `<person>.<key>.<HMAC under SECRET_KEY>` and never expires. Its GET changes nothing (link scanners open links); its POST, public, is the one write the same-origin check lets another site send: a mail client sends no Origin of this site, and the token is the proof.
  6. **Retention** (the owner's answer): `kernel.prune_notifications`, nightly, removes a notice 90 days after it's read, and any notice after a year.
  7. **Routes** at `/api/kernel/notifications`: the list (SPEC 7's shape, with `unread`), read marks, preferences (keys of the modules on), and the unsubscribe pair.
- **Alternatives:** *A separate in-site text per key in module.json:* a second wording to keep in step with the email's subject, for no gain. *A global "no notice emails" switch:* the owner chose per key only. *A one-click link that stops every notice email:* broader than RFC 8058 intends, and a surprise for one click. *Digests:* later (a non-goal). *Unsubscribe on GET:* mail scanners would unsubscribe people by opening the link.
- **Consequences:** SPEC 6's row, 14.6 (security emails are never notices) and the change log change. `kernel.notifications` joins the public surface; `KERNEL_JOBS` gains `kernel.prune_notifications`; migration `kernel_0009` adds the two tables, which go with the account by cascade. The footer links point at pages K4 serves (`/notifications/unsubscribe`, `/account/notifications`). Thread muting is the owning module's; push, SMS and translations are left for later.
