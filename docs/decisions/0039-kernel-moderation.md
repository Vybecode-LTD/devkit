# 0039: Kernel moderation: reports on declared kinds, one case per thing, filters that hold, bans as suspensions

- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Status:** Applied
- **Context:** SPEC 6 promised `moderation.report / queue / act` for K3: reports, a staff queue, word and link filters, and actions (hide, delete, time out, ban), all audited, shared by `comments`, `community` and any later forum or reviews (the Vibrant Mindz module plan, section 10). SPEC 11 has moderation emit `auth.user.suspended`. Decision 0031 declared `kernel.moderation.act` (admin, editor) and left who may report to K3. Decision 0037 promised moderators a narrower view of the audit log. Of KICKOFF's sources, VybeMindz's `moderation-audit-log` gave the pattern (guard, change and record in one transaction; nobody acts on themselves or above them; sessions end at once). VybeSite's `contact_filter.py` gave the filters' rule (held, never thrown away, the same answer to the sender), but it is a bot check, and `blocklist_service.py` checks DNS blocklists for mail reputation, so the word and link filters were built new. The contract is `dev plans/contracts/k3-moderation.md`.
- **Decision** (the owner approved the contract's goal and non-goals, with all five recommended answers):
  1. **What can be reported: kinds a module declares.** `@moderation.kind("<id>.<entity>", actions=("hide", "delete"))` on a `moderation.Kind` with `describe` (the thing now, or None), `apply` (hide or delete it, in the caller's transaction) and `dismissed` (show again what the filters held, when staff find nothing wrong; nothing by default), listed on `InstalledModule(reportable=...)`. The start refuses a kind its module doesn't own, one declared twice, and one missing either method. The kernel has no profiles, so a profile is its module's kind, with no actions of its own.
  2. **One case per thing.** `report` opens the thing's case or adds to the open one: a partial unique index keeps one open, the upsert takes concurrent reports, and a person reports a case once. The case keeps the excerpt (1,000 characters) and link as first reported, so the queue shows what was reported after an edit or a delete. A new report after a case closes opens a new one.
  3. **Who reports, who sees.** Signed-in accounts with a verified address, 20 reports an hour, never guests; the answer is the same for a first report and a repeat. The queue, each case's reports with who made them, and the moderators' log go to holders of `kernel.moderation.act`.
  4. **Who bans.** A ban suspends the account, so it needs `auth.user.suspend` (admins by default) as well. Editors dismiss, hide, delete and time out. This narrows 0031's description ("hide, delete, time out and ban"); a site can grant editors `auth.user.suspend`.
  5. **Guards.** Nobody acts on a case about what they wrote. Staff aren't timed out or banned from the queue: they're suspended from the People page. Staff's content is hidden or deleted only by someone holding every permission their roles carry, and an owner's only by an owner (decision 0013's rule; the `member` role counts for nothing, so a module's permission for members never stops an admin).
  6. **Filters now: hold, never drop.** The settings section `kernel.moderation` (`held_terms` up to 500, whole-word after NFKC and case folding; `blocked_domains`, subdomains included, in links, addresses or bare; `max_links`, default 2). `screen` gives the verdict and its reasons; the module keeps held text from others and calls `hold`, which opens a case whose report is the filters' (`reason` `filter`, no reporter). Nothing is automatic beyond that.
  7. **Time-outs** run an hour to 30 days (`auth_user.timed_out_until`), from a case or without one (live chat), and can be lifted; the person still signs in and reads. `Depends(moderation.can_post)` answers 403 `kernel.timed_out` with `until`.
  8. **Suspension** (`POST /api/auth/admin/users/{id}/suspend` and `/restore`, as `docs/contracts/auth-api.md` already had them, with `auth.user.suspend` and step-up, or a ban): status and a new session epoch, so every session ends and stays ended after a restore; audited, mailed, and emitted as `auth.user.suspended` and the new `auth.user.restored`. Nobody suspends themselves (403 `auth.not_yourself`) and only an owner suspends an owner (403 `auth.role_not_yours`, as for roles), which together keep the last owner. Both rows are locked in id order and the actor must still be active (409 `auth.account_not_active`), so two owners suspending each other at once can't both commit (CodeRabbit on #71). A ban from the queue needs a recent sign-in too.
  9. **Telling people.** A hide or a delete sends the notice `kernel.content_removed` (in the site and by email by default, then the person's choice); a time-out and a ban send mails nobody can turn off. Each names the reason and asks for a reply to the email if it's a mistake, never naming the reporter or the moderator. The reporter isn't told the outcome. No appeals in v1.
  10. **Audit and retention.** Seven kernel actions (`kernel.moderation.dismissed`, `.hidden`, `.deleted`, `.timed_out`, `.time_out_lifted`, `auth.user.suspended`, `auth.user.restored`), details holding the case and reason, never the note or excerpt. Reports aren't audited: their table is their record. `kernel.prune_moderation` deletes cases closed more than `AUDIT_KEEP_DAYS` ago, with their reports.
- **Alternatives:** *Modules report through their own routes only:* every module would rebuild the same route and checks; one kernel route and one `<ReportButton>` serve them all, and `report` stays callable for a module's own flows. *Deliveries to modules as events:* hiding would happen after the case closed, at least once and possibly while the module is off; `apply` in the same transaction closes the case exactly when the thing is hidden. *Editors ban:* a ban ends someone's account, which the kernel already keeps to `auth.user.suspend`. *Filters later:* comments and community need them at the site's step 3, before which the kernel phases end. *Drop held text:* a real post lost to a false match costs more than a spam post held.
- **Consequences:** SPEC 6, 7, 11, 14.7, 14.10 and 14.11 change, with a change-log line. Migration `kernel_0010` adds `kernel_moderation_case`, `kernel_moderation_report` and `auth_user.timed_out_until`. The kernel's notification key lives in code (`moderation.NOTIFICATIONS`): `kernel.json`'s schema has no `notifications`, and `notifications.preferences` always lists the kernel's keys. K4 builds the queue page, suspension on the People page, the filter settings and the kit's `<ReportButton>`; the community module closes a suspended person's open WebSocket on `auth.user.suspended`; K2 step 11 erases a deleted person's reports and excerpts.
